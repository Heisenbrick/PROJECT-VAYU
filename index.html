The Divide

The Problem

Delivery logistics for anything heavier than a small parcel is still stuck on the ground. Consumer drone delivery today tops out at a few kilograms — enough for a phone case, not a multi-item order or a full appliance. Everything above that weight still moves by truck, through traffic, on ground routes that don't scale with e-commerce demand from platforms like Amazon and Flipkart. There's an open gap between what trucks carry and what today's delivery drones can lift.

The Solution

Project VAYU is a VTOL cargo drone built around four ducted, counter-rotating tilt-fans mounted on a fixed wing spar. The same propulsion pods that lift it straight up rotate forward once airborne, trading vertical-lift efficiency for horizontal cruise efficiency — inside a 3×3 m footprint small enough for tight urban pads, carrying up to 80 kg. An onboard AI pilot runs the entire flight: takeoff, the tilt transition, cruise, obstacle avoidance, and landing. The operator's only input is a destination.

What Makes This Hard

"The interesting engineering problem here isn't lift — it's the transition."

01 // MECHANICS

Tilt Transition

The ducts rotate 90°→0° while airborne; the AI has to keep the drone stable as lift shifts from rotor thrust to aerodynamic lift, with no human in the loop.

02 // PHYSICS

Counter-Rotating Torque Cancellation

The left duct spins CW, the right spins CCW, so net yaw torque on the airframe stays near zero through the whole transition.

03 // DESIGN

Ducted Shrouding

The fans sit inside ducts, cutting tip-vortex noise for flying over residential routes and adding thrust efficiency compared to an open propeller design.

04 // SOFTWARE

Autonomous Path Planning

Real-time obstacle- and wind-compensated routing, so a destination is the only thing needed to be inputted by operators.

Roadmap

Short-Term

Build Week (7 Days)

The Vanguards Build Week is a one week proof of concept, not a manufacturing cycle, so the scope is deliberately three concrete, demonstrable pieces:

Flight-dynamics simulation Validating the vertical-to-horizontal tilt transition before anything physical gets committed to. A first working version, modeling flight phases, live gyroscopic torque, and telemetry, is already in progress.
Structural CAD design The frame, wing spar, and duct/tilt-mechanism geometry, finalized and ready to start prototype fabrication post-event.
Early-stage AI Pilot model Reads wind speed, wind direction, and obstacle data, and outputs propeller angle, thrust, optimum altitude, and a flight path, from a single destination input.

The target is a fully physical VTOL cargo drone with an AI pilot that handles flight operation and the transition phase seamlessly — reliable enough for real commercial delivery routes. Post-event, the first-year plan is to turn this into a working physical prototype and grow it into a startup.

Long-Term

Beyond Build Week

Why This, Why Now

Most student drone projects stop at a multirotor with a camera bolted on. This is a real aerospace control problem — tilt-transition dynamics, torque-cancelled counter-rotation, autonomous flight planning — scoped into something a small team can meaningfully move forward in a week, with a concrete path to a physical prototype and a startup beyond it.

The Team

We're building a small, high-execution crew rather than a large group. For a 7-day build with three concrete deliverables, a few people who can each own a piece end-to-end move faster than a large team that needs coordinating.
